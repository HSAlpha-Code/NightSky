# Plain-Language Glossary (`GLOSSARY.md`)

Every technical or astronomy term used across the NightSky ELI5 guides, explained in one plain sentence:

- **Accelerometer**: A tiny chip inside your phone that feels which direction Earth's gravity is pulling so the app knows which way is "up" and "down."
- **Altitude (Alt)**: How high an object sits in your local sky, measured in degrees from the flat horizon (`0°`) straight up to overhead (`+90°`) or down into the ground (`-90°`).
- **API (Application Programming Interface)**: A menu of web addresses (like `/api/solve`) that lets the phone app ask the backstage server questions and get predictable answers back.
- **Azimuth (Az)**: Compass direction around the horizon measured in degrees clockwise from true North (`0°` = North, `90°` = East, `180°` = South, `270°` = West).
- **Base64**: A way of spelling binary picture bytes using normal keyboard letters (`A–Z`, `a–z`, `0–9`, `+`, `/`) so an image can ride inside a text-based JSON message.
- **Bilinear filtering**: Smoothing out blocky pixels by blending the colors of the four closest neighbor pixels together.
- **B−V color index**: A single number astronomers use to measure a star's surface temperature — negative numbers are hot blue-white stars and positive numbers are cooler yellow, orange, or red stars.
- **Cache**: A fast short-term memory cubby where the app or server saves finished answers and downloaded pictures so asking again takes milliseconds instead of re-downloading.
- **CAR projection (Plate Carrée)**: Unrolling a round globe onto a flat rectangle where longitude runs evenly left-to-right and latitude runs evenly bottom-to-top.
- **Cross product**: A 3D geometry move that takes two arrows (like "Up" and "Magnetic North") and produces a third arrow pointing straight out at 90° to both ("East").
- **CSRF (Cross-Site Request Forgery)**: A browser trick where an unrelated website tries to send sneaky commands to another site or local tool you have open.
- **Decompression bomb**: A tiny compressed image file crafted to expand into gigabytes of raw pixels and crash a computer's memory if unpacked blindly.
- **Declination (Dec)**: Sky latitude — how far north (`+`) or south (`−`) of Earth's equator a star sits on the celestial sphere, in degrees (`-90°` to `+90°`).
- **Django**: A popular Python toolkit for building organized, secure web servers.
- **Dot product**: Multiplying two 3D unit arrows together to measure how closely they point in the same direction (`1.0` = identical, `0.0` = 90° apart, `-1.0` = opposite).
- **DSO (Deep-Sky Object)**: Any space sight beyond single stars and our Solar System — star clusters, glowing gas nebulae, supernova remnants, and whole galaxies.
- **ENU (East-North-Up)**: A local 3D coordinate box centered on your feet where `X` points East, `Y` points North, and `Z` points straight Up.
- **Ephemeris**: A calculated timetable showing where moving Solar System worlds (the Sun, Moon, and planets) sit in the sky at any chosen date and minute.
- **ETag (Entity Tag)**: A short version fingerprint stamped on server data so the phone can ask *"Has anything changed since this tag?"* and get a tiny `304 Not Modified` thumbs-up if not.
- **EXIF**: Hidden note tags saved inside a camera JPEG file (such as whether you held the phone upright or sideways when shooting).
- **Exponential backoff**: Waiting a little longer before each retry when an outside server is temporarily busy so you don't pile onto a traffic jam.
- **Field of View (FOV)**: How wide a slice of sky fits from the left edge to the right edge of your screen or camera photo, measured in degrees.
- **Fingerprint hash**: A short code (like `a3f9...`) computed from data that changes immediately if even a single letter inside the data changes.
- **Flutter**: Google's toolkit for building smooth, hardware-accelerated mobile apps in the Dart language.
- **Gamma / Soft-knee tone curve**: Gently dimming blindingly bright highlights in a photo while keeping dark and medium details visible and colorful.
- **GLSL (OpenGL Shading Language)**: A mini programming language for writing tiny math programs (**shaders**) that run on a phone's graphics chip for millions of pixels at once.
- **GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)**: The specialized chip inside your phone designed to paint textures, triangles, and shaders 60 times every second.
- **Gyroscope**: A motion chip that senses how fast your phone twists in midair for extra-smooth sky tracking.
- **HEALPix**: A clever math grid used by astronomers to slice a round sphere into equal-area diamond patches at zoomable detail levels.
- **HiPS (Hierarchical Progressive Surveys)**: An astronomy standard (like Google Maps for space) that stores huge sky surveys and Hubble/Webb photographs as zoomable pyramids of HEALPix tiles.
- **HTTP headers**: Short label lines (`Key: Value`) attached to the outside of a web response envelope carrying extra info like image credits.
- **ICRS / J2000**: The standard permanent star-map coordinate grid frozen to the sky of January 1, 2000, so every star catalog in the world agrees on star addresses.
- **Isolate**: A separate background worker brain in Dart/Flutter that crunches heavy tasks (like shrinking a 12 MP photo) without freezing screen animations.
- **JPEG / WebP**: Compact file formats that compress photographs so they take up much less storage and download faster.
- **JSON (JavaScript Object Notation)**: A human-readable text format (`{"key": "value"}`) computers use to pass structured notes back and forth.
- **Kepler's equation**: Johannes Kepler's 400-year-old orbit formula that turns a planet's travel time into its exact position along its oval path around the Sun.
- **Light-time**: The minutes or hours sunlight takes to bounce off a planet and travel across space to reach your eyes on Earth.
- **Light-year**: The distance a beam of light travels in one full year — about 9.46 trillion kilometers (5.88 trillion miles).
- **Low-pass filter**: A smoothing formula that blends each new sensor reading gently into the previous one so tiny hand tremors don't make the screen jitter.
- **Magnitude (Apparent Magnitude)**: Astronomy's backward golf-score brightness scale where **smaller (or negative) numbers mean brighter** (`Sirius = -1.46`, faintest naked-eye stars in dark skies `≈ +6.5`).
- **Magnetic declination**: The angle difference between magnetic North (where a compass needle points) and true geographic North (Earth's spin axis) at your location.
- **Magnetometer**: The digital compass chip inside your phone that senses Earth's magnetic field.
- **MAST**: NASA/STScI's *Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes*, the catalog where Hubble and James Webb observations are indexed.
- **Messier catalog (`M1`–`M110`)**: A famous list of 110 bright star clusters, nebulae, and galaxies compiled by comet-hunter Charles Messier.
- **OpenNGC**: A free community-maintained catalog of ~14,000 NGC and IC nebulae, galaxies, and star clusters.
- **Parallax**: The tiny shift in a nearby star's or the Moon's apparent position when viewed from two different places (across Earth's orbit, or from Earth's surface vs. Earth's center).
- **Platform channel**: A private communication wire inside the app that lets Flutter's Dart code talk directly to native Android (`Kotlin`) hardware APIs.
- **Plate solving**: Figuring out the exact sky coordinates and tilt of a photograph by matching geometric star patterns in the image against a star catalog.
- **Precession**: The slow 26,000-year wobble of Earth's spin axis (like a spinning toy top), which gradually shifts which way Earth's North Pole points among the stars.
- **Quaternion**: A four-number math package `[x, y, z, w]` that describes any 3D tilt smoothly without ever getting stuck when pointing straight up or straight down.
- **Rate limiting (Throttling)**: A polite speed limit at the server's front door (e.g. `20` heavy image requests per minute per IP) so one caller can't hog the whole lab.
- **Redshift**: How much light from a distant galaxy has been stretched toward red wavelengths by the expansion of the universe, used to estimate its distance.
- **Right Ascension (RA)**: Sky longitude — how far east around the sky a star sits (`0°` to `360°`, often written on a 24-hour clock like `05h 35m 17s`).
- **Riverpod**: The state-management library NightSky uses as a shared live bulletin board so screens automatically redraw when settings or server answers arrive.
- **Rotation matrix**: A 3×3 grid of 9 numbers that rotates a 3D arrow from the phone's body frame into the real world's East-North-Up frame.
- **Sidereal time**: Star-clock time measuring Earth's rotation relative to distant stars (`23h 56m 04s` per turn) rather than the Sun, telling which constellations are overhead right now.
- **TAN projection (Gnomonic projection)**: Projecting a curved sphere onto a flat plane from the center of the sphere — the exact geometry of a rectilinear camera lens.
- **Terminator**: The curved shadow line separating the sunlit day side and dark night side on the Moon or a planet.
- **`tetra3`**: A super-fast open-source star-pattern plate-solving library originally built by the European Space Agency (`ESA`) for satellite star trackers.
- **Topocentric parallax**: Adjusting the Moon's sky position for where *you* stand on Earth's surface rather than Earth's center (up to ~`1°` of difference!).
- **Vercel**: The cloud hosting platform where NightSky's Python Django backend runs on demand.
- **White top-hat filter**: An image-processing step that subtracts flat background brightness blocks while keeping sharp stars and delicate nebula threads, erasing patchwork seams between telescope exposures.
- **WMM-2025 (World Magnetic Model)**: NOAA's mathematical map of Earth's magnetic field, bundled inside NightSky so compass headings convert to true North completely offline.
